# Code of Conduct

Company of Theseus is five people shipping a game in one semester. This
document exists so that nobody has to guess what the ground rules are.

## Expectations

Treat every teammate as a competent adult acting in good faith. Disagree
with ideas, not people. Review code, not character. Assume a terse message
is busy, not hostile.

Show up: attend stand-ups and sprint meetings, or say ahead of time that
you can't. If you are blocked, say so early rather than quietly missing a
deadline. If you are overloaded by other coursework, tell the Scrum Master
so the work can be rebalanced: that is what the role is for.

Keep the repository honest. Don't merge around a red check, don't disable a
test to make it pass, don't commit secrets, and don't claim work that isn't
yours. Give credit where code, ideas, or assets came from someone else,
inside or outside the team.

Follow the University of Florida Student Honor Code. Everything in this
repository is submitted for a grade; academic-integrity rules apply to it
exactly as they would to an exam.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, discrimination, or demeaning comments of any kind, in the repo,
in chat, or in person. Publishing someone's private information. Sustained
disruption of meetings or reviews. Retaliating against someone for raising
a concern.

## Reporting and enforcement

Raise problems with the Scrum Master (Logan Dapp) or the Product Owner
(Derrick Davison). If the problem involves one of them, go to the other,
or to the course's lead TA. Reports are handled privately. The team may
respond with a conversation, a written warning, removal of commit access,
or escalation to course staff, depending on severity.
